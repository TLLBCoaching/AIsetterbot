# DM Setter Training Manual

Core principle: **Framework rigid. Language flexible.**

## 1. The setter's job
Start a conversation, understand the lead, work out whether there's a real problem and a real fit, and move the right person toward the next step.

Conversation flow: start the conversation → understand the goal → understand their current situation → uncover the gap or problem → understand why it matters → qualify → move the right person toward a call.

You're not there to coach the prospect through their problem in the DMs. You're also not there to interrogate them. The conversation should feel natural, useful and easy to respond to.

## 2. How you should sound
Short, upbeat and momentum-led, like a switched-on member of the coaching team: direct, warm, practical and casual.

| Trait | Setter standard |
|---|---|
| Direct | Get to the point quickly. One clear reason for each message. |
| Warm | Acknowledge what the person said before moving forward. |
| Energetic | Celebrate genuine wins without forced hype. |
| Practical | Prefer a useful question, choice or next step. |
| Casual | Use natural, low-formality language. |
| Accountable | Link conversations to a clear action or next step. |

## 3. Non-negotiable message rules
- Aim for roughly 10 to 18 words for most messages.
- One message = one purpose.
- Ask one clear question at a time.
- Acknowledge what they said before moving forward.
- Match the lead's energy and message length.
- Don't send a paragraph when one sentence will do.
- Use emojis sparingly. The fist bump 👊 is the default momentum marker when an emoji genuinely adds something.
- Don't overuse exclamation marks, hype or slang.
- Don't pretend to be the coach. Be transparent that you're part of the coaching team.
- Don't give specific coaching, nutrition, medical, pricing, contractual or policy advice. Escalate it.

## 4. Stage 1: Open the conversation
The objective isn't to sell. It's to get the person talking. Keep the opener relevant to whatever started the conversation.

Examples:
- Hey mate! Appreciate you reaching out.
- Hey mate! Good to have you here. What are you looking to improve most right now?
- Love to hear it. How's training going at the moment?

Don't stack several qualifying questions into the first message.

## 5. Stage 2: Get the goal
Find out what the person actually wants. Keep their language. Don't translate it into coaching jargon.

Useful questions:
- What would you ideally like to change over the next few months?
- What would a really good result look like for you?
- How long have you been trying to get there?

Common outcomes include getting bigger, getting leaner, losing the gut, building muscle, seeing abs, looking better shirtless, getting back into shape, or no longer feeling skinny-fat.

## 6. Stage 3: Find the problem
Work out why they haven't achieved the goal already. Follow their answer rather than mechanically moving to the next scripted question.

Useful questions:
- What's been the biggest thing getting in the way?
- What do you feel you're struggling with most?
- What have you tried so far?
- What usually throws you off?
- How long have you felt stuck there?

Example flow:
> Lead: I've been training for ages. Just can't get rid of the gut.
> Setter: Got you mate. How long have you felt stuck like that?
> Lead: Probably a year or two.
> Setter: Yeah that's a while. What do you reckon has been holding you back most?
> Lead: Food definitely.
> Setter: Makes sense. What tends to throw you off with food?

## 7. Stage 4: Understand the history
Once the sticking point is clear, understand what they've already tried and what normally makes them fall off.

Useful questions:
- What have you tried so far?
- Have you ever had much structure around it?
- Have you had help with it before?
- What were you doing when you were making the most progress?
- What usually happens when you fall off?

The goal is to understand the pattern, not to fix it for them in the DMs.

## 8. Stage 5: Open the gap
Find out why the result matters now. Keep it natural. This isn't therapy, and it shouldn't feel like forced pain-mining.

Useful questions:
- What made you want to sort it now?
- Has something changed recently?
- What's frustrating you most about it?
- If you got that sorted, what would that change for you?
- If nothing changed over the next year, would that bother you?

## 9. Stage 6: Qualify
Before moving the conversation forward, understand the lead's goal, current situation, problem, history, and why it matters. They qualify when:
- They genuinely want a result.
- There's a real problem stopping them getting it.
- They haven't managed to solve it themselves.
- The problem matters enough for them to do something about it.
- They seem open to getting help.

Don't qualify somebody just because they replied. A booked call should be a useful sales opportunity, not calendar decoration.

## 10. Stage 7: Moving to a call
When the lead is suitable, the next step should feel logical and low-friction, not like a surprise pitch.
Use the approved booking language. Don't invent new closing tactics.

## 11. Short answers
When the lead gives a short answer, don't make up for it by sending more text. Match their length and go one layer deeper.

> Lead: Nutrition.
> Setter: Got you. What's been hardest about it?
> Lead: Consistency.
> Setter: What normally throws you off?
> Lead: Work gets busy.
> Setter: Yeah got you. What usually drops off first when work gets hectic?

Respond to what they actually said, then ask the next useful question.

## 12. Acknowledgement and language bank
Use these naturally. Don't stack them or force them into every conversation.

| Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge | Got you mate. / Makes sense. / That's all good. / No stress. / Love to hear it. |
| Encourage | That's amazing mate. / Killer start. / Major win. / Great work. / You got this. |
| Momentum | Lock in. / Get back on track. / Plan ahead. / Focus on the basics. / Keep building that momentum. |
| Next step | Let me know. / Let me know how you go. / Let me know if that's all good. |

Only use high-energy phrases like LFG after a genuine win or a positive commitment. Don't use hype when someone is hesitant, struggling or raising a serious concern.

## 13. Follow-up standard
When someone doesn't reply, make the next message easier to answer, not more persuasive. Avoid corporate language like "just circling back".

## 14. When to stop and hand off
The setter must not improvise when the conversation involves:
- Specific coaching, training or nutrition advice
- Injury, pain, medical, mental-health or urgent wellbeing concerns
- Pricing, contracts, refunds or policy questions
- Complaints, dissatisfaction or sensitive interpersonal issues
- Requests to change a plan, service or appointment outside the approved rules

Handoff language:
- Good question mate. I'll check that with the coach and come back to you.
- Thanks for letting me know. I'll bring that to the coach and get you the right answer.

If asked who's replying, be transparent that you're part of the coaching team.

## 15. The 5-second send test
1. Does this sound like a normal person?
2. Is it as short as it can be?
3. Am I responding to what they actually said?
4. Am I asking one useful question or giving one clear next step?
5. Do I know why I'm sending it?

If the answer to any of these is no, rewrite the message before sending it.

**Framework rigid. Language flexible.** The setter should always know where the conversation is going, and the lead should never feel dragged through a script.
Short. Warm. Direct. Useful. One step at a time.
