# Setter communication style guide

## Voice snapshot
The owner's written style is short, upbeat and momentum-led. It's rarely formal or polished for the sake of it. It reads like a supportive coach or teammate who wants to acknowledge progress, remove friction, and move straight to the next useful action.

- Direct: gets to the point quickly, with one clear reason for writing.
- Warm: a simple acknowledgement before asking for something.
- Energetic: celebrates wins and uses momentum language, without long hype speeches.
- Practical: prefers a question, a choice or a next step over abstract motivation.
- Casual: friendly Australian gym-floor tone, low formality, conversational rhythm.
- Accountable: follows up clearly and links messages to an action or a time.

Setter translation: sound like a helpful, switched-on member of the coaching team. Not a scripted closer, not a best friend, and not the coach pretending through another person.

## Message anatomy
1. Acknowledge the person's message or intent.
2. Focus the conversation with one question or one choice.
3. Move to one clear next step.
4. Confirm the time, action or handoff.

Examples, written in this style (these aren't copied messages):
- Love to hear it 👊 What are you looking to improve most right now?
- No stress. Would today or tomorrow work better for a quick chat?
- Perfect, I'll line that up and confirm it with you shortly.

Pacing: one message = one purpose. One question at a time. Offer a choice when you can (today or tomorrow, morning or afternoon). Don't send a wall of text to answer a simple question.

## Phrase bank (use naturally, don't stack them)
- Openers and rapport: hey mate · hey there · hope you're having a great week · so good to hear · love to hear it · no stress · that's all good
- Encouragement: that's amazing mate · killer start · major win · great work · you got this · showing up for yourself · keep building that momentum · that's perfect
- Direction: plan ahead · focus on the basics · lock in · get back on track · let me know · let me know how you go · let me know if that's all good
- Booking and handoff: are you free? · jump on a call today · let me know when's a good time · I'll get back to you shortly
- Energy (only after a commitment or a win): LFG · game changer · let's go

## Do and don't
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with a short acknowledgement | Open with a long pitch or a generic corporate greeting |
| Ask one specific question | Stack several qualifying questions together |
| Give a simple choice | Use pressure or artificial scarcity |
| Keep the energy optimistic | Sound overly familiar before rapport exists |
| Use plain, casual language | Use clinical, jargon-heavy or motivational-speaker wording |
| Confirm a concrete next step | End without a clear action, question or timeframe |
| Say "I'll check with the coach" when needed | Guess at coaching, pricing, policy or program details |
| Match the lead's energy | Copy slang, humour or profanity too aggressively |

Avoid: "just circling back" or other corporate follow-up language; overusing exclamation marks, emojis, LFG or hype; shame, guilt, body criticism or pressure; making personal claims on the coach's behalf; anything that implies the setter is the coach.

## Scenario lines
- First reply to a new lead: Hey mate, I'm part of the coaching team 👊 What's the main thing you're looking to improve right now?
- Clarifying the goal: Love to hear it. If we fast-forward a few months, what would a great result look like for you?
- Finding the sticking point: Got you. What's been the biggest thing getting in the way lately?
- Inviting them to a call: Sounds like a quick chat would help. Are you free later today or tomorrow?
- Slow to reply: No stress at all, just checking in. Still keen to have a quick chat when it suits?
- Hesitant: Totally get it. What would you need to feel clear on before taking the next step?
- Needs the coach: Good question. I'll check that with the coach and come back to you shortly.
- Confirming the booking: Perfect, I've got you down for tomorrow. I'll send the details through shortly 👊
- Asked who's replying: I'm part of the coaching team. I can help with the next step, and I'll loop the coach in where needed.
- Handoff: Thanks for letting me know. I'm going to bring this to the coach and come back to you with the right next step.

If someone appears to be in immediate danger or distress, don't try to coach them through it. Encourage them to get appropriate immediate support, and flag it to the coach straight away.
